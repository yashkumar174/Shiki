# Shiki

*Formerly Converted Bro. Updating from it moves your settings, history, keys, models, results folder, Explorer menu and start-with-Windows entry over by itself the first time Shiki starts.*

A little pixel cat for Windows that converts files without opening a browser.
Select a file and press Ctrl+Alt+X, Shift + drag it anywhere and drop it on a format, drop it on the cat, or right-click it in Explorer.
Everything runs locally: nothing is uploaded anywhere. The one exception is *Ask the cat*, which you switch on with your own Claude API key (see below).

Inspired by [Tangerine](https://x.com/th2049) for macOS.

## What it does

The wheel has up to five pages: **formats** (what to convert to), **tools** (what to do to the file), **size limit** (make it fit an upload limit), **form photos** (exact sizes for exam, passport and visa forms) and **files** (zip anything, folders included).

| Files | Formats | Tools | Size limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Images (png, jpg, webp, heic, avif, gif, bmp, tiff, ico, svg) | PNG, JPG, WEBP, ICO, PDF (all images into one A4 PDF) | Remove background, Scan, Copy text, Read QR, Copy image, Compress, Half size, Rotate, Remove metadata (camera, date, GPS) | 100 KB, 200 KB, 1 MB, 2 MB; form photos: Exam photo, Passport, Visa, Signature |
| Video (mp4, mov, mkv, avi, webm, …) | MP4, WEBM, GIF, MP3 | Compress (up to 1080p), Half size, Rotate, Mute | |
| Audio (mp3, wav, m4a, flac, ogg, …) | MP3, M4A, WAV, FLAC, OGG | Even out volume | |
| PDF | Pages to PNG, Pages to JPG, Word (with Word 2013+) | Copy text, Edit pages, Sign, Unlock, Lock, Page numbers, Split into pages, Merge (PDFs and images, in name order) | 100 KB, 200 KB, 1 MB, 2 MB |
| Documents (doc, docx, xls, xlsx, csv, ppt, pptx, odt, ods, odp, rtf) | PDF | | |
| Archives (zip, 7z, rar, tar, tar.gz, …) | | Extract here | |
| Anything, folders too | | Zip (on the Files page) | |

- **Size limit** finds the highest quality that fits (binary search on JPEG/WEBP quality, then a smaller picture if even the lowest quality is too big). PDFs are re-rendered page by page, so their pages become pictures.
- **Copy text** uses the OCR engine built into Windows (offline) and puts the text on the clipboard. It reads the languages installed in Windows.
- **Snip and convert** (Ctrl+Shift+Q, changeable in Settings): the screen freezes, drag a box, and the wheel opens on the snip. Snips are saved in `Pictures\Shiki`. Copy text on a snip reads text from anything on screen.
- **Edit pages**: thumbnails of every page (of one or several PDFs); drag to reorder, turn or remove, then save one PDF.
- **Sign**: draw your signature once (or use a photo of it on paper; the paper is removed), then click to place it on any page, drag to move, drag the corner to resize. It's a visual signature, not a certificate.
- **Unlock / Lock**: remove a known password (the dialog checks it straight away), or add one (AES-256). Unusual encryption that PDFsharp can't rewrite is unlocked by re-rendering the pages.
- **Page numbers**: "3 / 10" centred at the bottom of each page.
- **Remove background**: runs offline on your own PC with BiRefNet (MIT). The model (115 MB) downloads the first time you use it, to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Shiki\models`. One photo opens an editor: the cut-out on a checkerboard, a background swatch row (transparent, white, black, grey, blue, red, or any colour), and a touch-up brush (Erase / Restore, S–XL, `[` `]` to resize, Ctrl+Z to undo, scroll to zoom, right-drag to pan, "Hold to compare" for the original). Save gives `name (no background).png`, or a JPG on a colour; Copy puts it on the clipboard. Several photos at once are cut out straight to transparent PNGs. The mask is refined at full resolution (guided filter) and the old background's colour is cleaned out of soft edges, so hair and fur don't get a halo. It uses the graphics card through DirectML when it can (about 4 s a photo on a GTX 1650; the first one after a while takes longer while the card warms up) and the processor otherwise.
- **Form photos**: Exam photo (3.5 × 4.5 cm, 20–50 KB), Passport (2 × 2 in), Visa (35 × 45 mm), Signature (3.5 × 1.5 cm, 10–20 KB). The crop starts centred on the face (Windows' built-in face detector, offline) or on the signature's ink; drag or scroll to adjust. The JPG gets the right pixel size and DPI, and the highest quality inside the size range (a tiny file is padded with an empty comment to reach a minimum). **White background** swaps whatever is behind you for plain white.
- **Paste onto the cat** (*Paste from clipboard* in its menu): copied files open the wheel, a copied picture (a screenshot, an image from a browser) is saved in `Pictures\Shiki` and opens the wheel, and copied text opens the QR maker. Every finished job's card has **Copy**: one picture pastes as a picture, anything else as files.
- **Record the screen** (Ctrl+Alt+R, changeable in Settings; also in the cat's menu and Tools): the screen dims (live, not frozen); drag a box, or press Enter for the whole screen. A dashed frame marks the area and a small bar offers **microphone** and **system sound** toggles (remembered) and **Record**. After a 3-2-1 countdown the frame turns red and the bar shows the time and **Stop** (or press Ctrl+Alt+R again). The frame and bar never appear in the video. It's saved as `Recording <date>.mp4` (results folder, or `Videos\Shiki`), goes into History, and the wheel opens on it, ready for GIF, compress and the rest. Capture uses Windows' GPU desktop duplication through FFmpeg (`ddagrab`) and the graphics card's encoder (NVENC) when there is one, falling back to GDI capture and x264. Sound is captured with WASAPI (NAudio) and mixed in at the end, with silent stretches filled so it stays in sync.
- **Office documents to PDF**: Word, Excel and PowerPoint files (and their OpenDocument cousins, RTF, CSV) through the Office on the PC, run hidden; LibreOffice is used when Office isn't installed. Apps that were already open with your documents are left running. **PDF to Word** needs Word 2013 or newer; on a PC with an old Office next to a new one the newest Word is used, an unlicensed Word gets a clear message instead of a hang, and Word's own "convert this PDF?" prompt is switched off only for the conversion and put back afterwards.
- **Pick a colour** (Ctrl+Alt+C, changeable in Settings): the screen freezes with a magnifier under the cursor; click to copy the hex (`#1E90FF`), and the card offers RGB and HSL too. Arrow keys nudge one pixel (Shift for ten), Enter picks, Esc cancels. It isn't Ctrl+Shift+C because terminals copy and browsers inspect with that.
- **QR codes**: *Make a QR code* in the cat's menu turns a link or any text into a QR code as you type (save as PNG or copy). **Read QR** on any picture or snip reads QR codes and barcodes (light-on-dark too), copies what they say and offers *Open link* for a web address.
- **Zip / Extract**: Zip packs files and whole folders into one `.zip` named after the first item (photos and videos are stored without recompressing, which is faster and no bigger). Extract unpacks zip, 7z, rar, tar and compressed tarballs next to the archive: a single folder inside comes out as itself, anything else goes into a folder named after the archive. Entries that try to escape the folder (`..\..\`) are skipped. Password-protected archives aren't supported.
- **Scan** opens an editor: auto-detect places four corners on the page (paper is bright and colourless: Otsu threshold, biggest blob, extreme corners), you drag them where needed (a loupe magnifies the corner), pick *Clean colour*, *Black and white* or *Original*, rotate, preview the result, and save one PDF or one image per photo. Cleanup straightens the page with a perspective warp and divides by a blurred copy of itself, which evens out shadows and turns the paper white.

Results never overwrite anything (`photo (1).jpg`, `clip (small).mp4`, `report (pages)\page-01.png`). Where they go is up to you, in Settings → **Where results go**:

- **One folder** (the default): everything lands in `C:\Users\<you>\Shiki` (outside OneDrive, so big videos don't get synced), or any folder you pick with *Change…*. Turn on **Sort by type** to get `Images`, `Videos`, `Audio`, `PDFs`, `Archives` and `Extracted` subfolders. Snips, pasted pictures and QR codes go there too. *Open results folder* is in the cat's menu.
- **Next to the original**: each result sits beside the file it came from (snips then go to `Pictures\Shiki`).

## The dashboard

Open it by launching Shiki.exe, clicking the tray icon, or with *Open Shiki* in the cat's menu. It's styled after Wispr Flow: a warm paper canvas or deep charcoal (the moon in the title bar switches; it starts out following Windows), white cards, Instrument Serif for the big moments, and one warm orange accent.

- **Home**: a greeting, the black card (drop files on it, choose some, or snip the screen), the cat's numbers (files converted, space saved, day streak), one tip for whatever isn't set up yet, and today's results with Open, Copy, Folder and "Do it again" on hover.
- **Tools**: every tool by kind of file. "Convert to" and "Shrink under" chips, then tiles; pick one and choose the files. Capture tools (snip, colour picker, QR, paste) are at the top.
- **Player**: videos and sound play right inside the dashboard: click a recording or any video/audio result in Home or History (or its play button). Play/pause, a seek bar you can click or drag, time, volume and mute, speed (1x, 1.5x, 2x, 0.5x) and full screen; Open (in your usual player), Copy, Show in folder and Convert (GIF, compress and the rest) at the top. Keys: Space or K, Left/Right 5 s, J/L 10 s, Up/Down volume, M mute, F full screen, Esc back. Step to another page and it pauses and waits in the sidebar. It uses Windows' own media engine, so it plays what Windows plays; anything else gets a message and an Open button. `Shiki.exe play <file>` opens it directly.
- **History**: every job ever, grouped by day, with search (Ctrl+F) and filters. Removing a line only forgets it; the files stay. Kept in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Shiki\history.json` (the newest 2,000).
- **Set up Shiki**: a checklist in the sidebar (Explorer menu, the Ctrl+Alt+X shortcut, results folder, start with Windows) that checks the real state and does each step when clicked. It goes away when everything's done.
- **Editors inside**: Pages, Sign, Remove background, Scan, Form photos, Lock/Unlock and the QR maker open as pages in the dashboard (in its look, with a back arrow), wherever you start them: the wheel, the cat, Explorer or Tools. Step to another page and the editor waits in the sidebar; opening a different editor replaces it. Esc goes back.
- **Settings** is a page too. Peeking at it never closes an open editor. Click the cat at the bottom of the sidebar to switch cats.

*Start with Windows* starts quietly (`--background`): just the cat, no window. `Shiki.exe dashboard [home|tools|history]` opens a page.

## Ask the cat (Claude)

Tell the cat what you want in plain English: *"merge the PDFs I downloaded today"*, *"put these photos in one PDF and shrink it under 1 MB"*, *"show me the videos on my desktop"*. Type on the **Ask** page in the dashboard (it's named after your cat), or press **Ctrl+Alt+A** anywhere for a small bubble above the cat (changeable in Settings). Or just talk: press the mic, or **Ctrl+Alt+V** anywhere (see Voice below). Or say **"Hey <cat name>"** (see Wake word below).

- **How it works**: Claude Haiku 5.5 reads the request and uses a few tools: find files (Downloads, Desktop, Documents, Pictures, Videos, Music, the results folder, the folder open in File Explorer, or any path; by kind, name words and age), read what's selected in File Explorer or on the desktop ("these"), open File Explorer with files highlighted ("show me"), open or play a file, and start the app's own tools (snip, record, colour picker, QR, paste).
- **Nothing changes without your OK**: to make files, the cat shows a plan card (one to six steps, built from the same actions as the wheel; a step can work on what the previous one made) with **Do it** and **Not now**. Results are new files as always; nothing is deleted, moved or renamed. Steps that need you (sign, edit pages, passwords, form photos) open their editor in the dashboard as the last step. Ask a follow-up ("now compress it") and the cat remembers what it made.
- **Your key, your data**: the API key is kept in Windows Credential Manager (Settings → *Ask the cat*, or the Ask page the first time), never in a file or the log. What goes to Anthropic: your words, file and folder names and paths the cat looks at, what's selected in Explorer, and pictures of your screen when you paw something or ask about the window in front. File contents are never sent. File names are treated as data, never as instructions. Switch it off in Settings and nothing is sent at all.
- **Cost**: a request is usually a tenth of a cent or less (Haiku 5.5 is $0.10 / $0.50 per million tokens in / out, with prompt caching). The Ask page shows what the current chat cost; Settings shows the running total.
- **Paw to ask** (**Ctrl+Alt+P**, the cat's menu, Tools, or "Hey Sky, let me show you something"): the screen holds still and the pointer becomes the cat's pixel paw. Swipe it around anything and it leaves a trail of paw prints in your cat's colours; let go and the bubble opens next to what you picked, with *What is this?*, *Copy the text*, *Make it a table* and *Explain it simply* (or type or say anything). A plain click picks the whole window under the paw. The picture goes to Claude with your question (shrunk to 1568 px at most). Text can be copied straight to the clipboard; tables come back as a card with **Copy** (pastes into Excel or Sheets as cells) and **Save as CSV**.
- **What's this error?** (**Ctrl+Alt+E**, the cat's menu, Tools, or just ask "what's this error?" / "what's on my screen?"): the cat takes a picture of the window in front (the app draws it itself, so the bubble on top isn't in it; Shiki's own windows, the desktop and the taskbar are skipped), then explains what went wrong in plain words with a few safe steps to fix it. It never suggests deleting files, turning off security or commands it can't explain.
- **Meeting buddy** ("Hey Sky, take notes", **Ctrl+Alt+N**, the cat's menu, or the Meetings page; it also offers once when Zoom, Teams, Meet in a browser or another calling app starts using the microphone): a small pill at the top shows the time, who's talking, **Mark** and **Stop**, and (opened) the words as they're written down. It never takes focus from the call and is left out of screen shares. Your microphone and what the PC plays are recorded as two channels, so "You" is always right; Deepgram (multichannel, diarized) tells the other voices apart as Speaker 1, 2… (name them on the meeting's page). Without Deepgram, Whisper on this PC writes it down in pieces. "Hey Sky, mark that" stars a moment; "Hey Sky, stop notes" ends it. Then Claude writes the notes (structured JSON): a title, summary, decisions, open questions, tasks with who, when and the words that made them, and a follow-up email. Your own tasks go straight to **Tasks**. The **Meetings** page lists every meeting (searchable by anything said), with Copy notes, Copy transcript, Copy email, *Ask Sky about it* (attaches the meeting to the chat) and Forget. Settings: keep the recording too (M4A, with recordings; off by default), offer notes when a call starts. Deepgram bills per channel, so notes cost about $0.0154 a minute (about $0.92 an hour) plus a fraction of a cent for the write-up.
- **Tasks**: from meetings, added on the Tasks page (with Today, Tomorrow, This weekend, In a week), or by telling the cat ("remind me to call the bank tomorrow at 5"). Overdue, Today, Coming up, Someday and Done. When one is due, a reminder card offers **Done**, **In an hour** and **Tomorrow** (9 am, or the time given). Kept in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Shiki\tasks.json`; meetings in `meetings\`.
- **Voice**: press the mic button (Ask page or bubble) or **Ctrl+Alt+V** and speak in English; the cat listens until you pause for about two seconds (so a pause to think between words doesn't cut you off; or press the key again, or Enter), writes it down and acts on it. When a spoken request ends in a plan, it listens once more: say "yes" / "do it" or "no" / "not now" to answer the card, or say something else to ask again. **Conversation mode**: after each spoken request and the reply, the cat keeps listening (8 seconds by default; Settings → Voice: off, 5, 8 or 15), so the next thing needs no "Hey <name>" and no button. Nothing is sent to Deepgram while it waits: it connects once this PC hears you start talking, and what you said meanwhile is sent once it's up. "Thanks", "that's all", "stop listening" or a quiet spell ends it; so does a long stretch of words while it waited, or a dozen turns in a row (more likely a video or other people talking), and the bubble goes away if you aren't using it. Speech is turned into text on this PC by Whisper (OpenAI's English base model through whisper.cpp / Whisper.net, MIT; 148 MB, downloaded the first time you talk to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Shiki\models`). It runs on the graphics card through Vulkan (NVIDIA, AMD or Intel) when there is one, and on the processor otherwise. The microphone is only on while it's listening. The speech engine is swappable, separately for what you say and for the wake word (Settings → *Voice*):
  - **On this PC** (Whisper, the default): free and private.
  - **Deepgram** (Nova-3, your own API key, kept in Credential Manager and checked before it's saved): commands stream live over a WebSocket, so your words appear in the box as you speak and Deepgram judges when you've finished (0.9 s of silence, or 1.2 s without new words); the microphone starts straight away and what you said while it connects is sent once it's up. For the wake word, this PC still notices when someone is talking and only those short bursts of speech (under six seconds) are sent, never silence; that does include anyone else talking nearby while listening is on. The cat's name goes in as a Deepgram key term. A check takes about 0.3 to 0.4 s once connected (connections are kept open for ten idle minutes). If Deepgram can't be reached, Whisper takes over. Settings shows the minutes sent so far.
  - **Wispr Flow** (for commands): its API is enterprise-only for now, so Whisper is used until that adapter can be written against their docs; its key can be saved already.
- **Wake word**: name your cat whatever you like and teach it: *Teach your cat its name* (in the setup checklist, Settings → Voice, or `Shiki.exe teach`) asks you to say "Hey <name>" three times (it saves by itself after the third) and remembers how Whisper writes it in your voice ("hey lottie", "hey latte"); nothing is trained in the machine-learning sense. With *Listen for "Hey <name>"* on, the microphone stays open (Windows shows its mic icon) and a small voice detector cuts it into bursts of speech; only bursts under six seconds are written down by Whisper on this PC (two threads, in the background) and checked against those spellings plus "hi", "hello" and "okay <name>", allowing one slip for most names and none for very short ones. What it hears is never saved or sent; long talk (calls, videos) is skipped unread. The model stays loaded (and warmed up) while listening is on; checks give Whisper the name as a hint so it spells it the same way each time, use half its usual window and stop after a few words, so "Hey Latte" is recognised in 0.1 to 0.3 s on the graphics card, about half a second after you finish saying it. "Hey Latte" opens the bubble listening; "Hey Latte, merge my PDFs" goes straight to the request. It pauses while the cat is already listening or thinking, while you record the screen, and while you teach it. Toggle it in Settings → Voice or the cat's menu.
- **Only your voice**: when you teach the cat its name, one extra sentence ("Hey Sky, this is my voice, only listen to me") and your three takes become a voiceprint: a speaker model on this PC (WeSpeaker ResNet34 trained on VoxCeleb, CC-BY 4.0, 27 MB, downloaded then; features computed in-app as Kaldi-style 80-bin log mel filterbanks) turns speech into 256 numbers that describe the voice, not the words. A wake word whose voice is too far from yours (cosine below 0.58; Settings → Voice: Relaxed 0.45, Normal, Strict 0.7) is ignored and logged. In a test, the voice it learned scored 0.75 to 0.95 and another voice 0.31 to 0.52; a check takes about 30 ms. Stored in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Shiki\voiceprint.json`; it never leaves the PC.
- **Dictation anywhere** (**Ctrl+Alt+D**): hold it and talk; let go and your words are typed where the cursor is, in any app (WhatsApp, Word, email, a browser). A quick tap listens until you pause instead (or press it again). A small pill above the taskbar shows the words as they come, and never takes the keyboard. It uses the same engine as commands (Deepgram live when chosen, Whisper otherwise), waits for Ctrl and Alt to be let go so nothing becomes a shortcut, and types each character as Unicode (any language, no clipboard). An app running as administrator won't take typing from a normal app; then the words go on the clipboard instead. Only the word count is logged.
- **Voice for the PC**: "mute", "volume 40", "turn it up", "brightness 70", "dark mode", "do not disturb", "open Spotify", "close Word", "switch to my headphones", "what's my battery?". The plain ones run straight away on the PC (no Claude call, so they're instant and free); anything else goes to Claude, which has the same controls. Opening only reaches apps in the Start menu; closing is like the app's own X button, so it can still ask to save. Lock, sleep, restart and shut down always show a card first and wait for Do it (or a spoken yes). Brightness works on laptop screens and on monitors that allow it (DDC/CI). Do not disturb uses Windows' own switch; if Windows refuses, the notification settings open instead.
- **The cat talks back**: when you speak to it, it answers out loud too (Settings: when I speak, always, or off), and its mouth moves while it talks. Voices: Deepgram's natural Aura voices with a Deepgram key (about $0.03 per 1,000 characters), or the Windows voices (free, on this PC); pick one in Settings to hear it. Long answers are cut to their first sentences ("the rest is on screen"), and paths and links aren't read out. It never hears itself: the follow-up listening and "Hey Sky" wait until it has finished, and pressing the mic cuts it short.
- **Routines**: a few things at once from one phrase. Two come ready: "start work mode" (Do not disturb on, your task list) and "done for the day" (notifications back on). Make your own by telling the cat, e.g. "when I say meeting, turn on do not disturb and open Zoom": steps can be PC controls, opening or closing apps, opening a website, taking notes, showing tasks, or a line for the cat to say. An app that isn't installed is turned into its website, or refused. Settings lists them with Run and Remove; say "change the meeting routine…" to edit one. Saying a routine's phrase runs it on the PC straight away, without a Claude call.
- **File chores by voice**: "tidy my downloads" sorts the loose files into Images, Videos, Music, PDFs, Documents, Archives and Installers (files still downloading, or of other kinds, stay put), with an Undo button and "undo that". "What's eating my disk?" checks every drive's free space, the usual folders, the Recycle Bin and the biggest files, and says what to do about them (it never deletes anything itself). "Back up my documents" copies a folder to another drive, into "Shiki backups", only what's new or changed since last time. "Copy the invoice to my USB drive" copies files anywhere without replacing anything. "Make a passport photo from my newest photo" finds it and opens the form-photo tool. Each one shows a card first.
- **Screen by voice**: "record my screen" and "stop recording", "copy the text on my screen" (the cat reads the window in front and puts the text on the clipboard, or a table as cells), and "what colour is this?" with the pointer on it (the colour's code, a plain name, and the code copied).
- **Documents**: ask about what's inside a file. "How much is the deposit in the rental agreement?", "summarise this Word file", "what did I spend on food in Expenses.xlsx?". The cat reads PDFs (scans too: it looks at the pages), Word .docx, PowerPoint .pptx, Excel .xlsx and text files, on this PC, and sends the words to Claude to answer. "Read it to me" reads a whole document aloud in the cat's voice, with a Stop button (and "Hey Sky, stop" works while it reads, once your voice is taught).
- **What is this?, smart rename, translate**: "what is this file?" or "is this safe to open?" gives the facts (kind, size, dates, where it was downloaded from, photo date and camera, video length, a program's publisher and whether Windows' signature check passes), and for a picture the cat looks at it too. "Rename these photos by what they show" or "give these PDFs proper names" looks at each file, then shows a card of old and new names, with Undo. "Translate this document into Hindi" makes a translated Word file (the card shows the cost first); text on screen or in a picture is translated in the chat. "Save that as a document" keeps a summary or translation as a Word file.
- **Clipboard magic, memory and habits**: "make what I copied into a table", "clean this up", "fix the spelling", "turn this address into a QR code": the cat reads the clipboard (text or a picture) and puts the result back, ready to paste. "Remember that my PDFs should be under 2 MB" (or "call me Yash") is kept for good and used from then on; "forget that" removes it. When you keep converting the same kind of download the same way (HEIC to JPG three times in two weeks), it offers to do it by itself for every new one in Downloads. Settings lists what it remembers and does by itself, with Forget and Stop.
- **Live captions and translation**: "captions on" shows subtitles above the taskbar for whatever the PC is playing (a video, a lecture, a call); "subtitles in Hindi" or "translate this video into English" adds a translation of each sentence, with the original small underneath. Only the speakers are listened to, never your microphone. With a Deepgram key it follows any of its languages live (about $0.0077 a minute, plus a fraction of a cent per translated line with Claude); without one, Whisper on this PC captions English a few seconds behind. Drag it anywhere; × or "captions off" closes it.
- **Suggestions, quips, privacy guard and data extraction**: a new download that's probably wanted another way (a HEIC photo, a huge video, an archive) gets a one-tap suggestion, worked out on this PC. Now and then the cat says a short line in a bubble (welcome back with today's tasks, how much a compression saved, a nudge to sleep at 2 am), at most once every 20 minutes and never over full-screen apps. The privacy guard hides card numbers, Aadhaar and PAN numbers, passwords and keys in documents and copied text before anything goes to Claude, and says what it hid. "A table of the dates and totals from these files" reads each one and fills a table to copy or save. Closing an app by voice now asks first. Each can be turned off in Settings.
- **Do it for me (the agent)**: "reply to Rahul's email saying I'll be there", "fill in this form", "find the cheapest train to Mumbai on Friday": after a card, the cat uses the mouse and keyboard itself (Claude Sonnet 5.5's computer use, usually a few cents a task), in the apps and websites you already use; email goes through the Gmail or Outlook already open in your browser, so there's nothing to connect. A pill at the top right shows what it's doing, with Stop. Before anything that can't be undone (sending, submitting, posting, deleting) it asks there, with Yes and No; the agent can't see or press that pill itself. It never types passwords, card or ID numbers, never pays or creates accounts, and asks you to do sign-ins yourself. Touch the real mouse or keyboard and it stops at once.
- **Hindi and Hinglish**: Settings → Voice → I speak → Hindi + English. Deepgram then follows Hindi and English as they mix in one sentence (Hinglish); Whisper on this PC switches to its multilingual model (190 MB, downloaded once). The quick commands understand Hinglish too ("awaaz kam karo", "volume chalis pe kar do", "Chrome kholo", "dark mode on karo", "captions chalu karo", "notes lena shuru karo", "ruko"), and so do the yes/no answers ("haan", "nahi") and goodbyes ("shukriya", "bas"). Claude understands all of it and answers in Hinglish when you speak, so the voice can read it out. Dictation can type Hindi in Devanagari or the Roman way (aap kaise ho).
- **The web and Gmail, fast**: questions about the world ("weather in Pune", "price of the iPhone 16", "what does this website say") are answered with Claude's own web search and page reading, in seconds, with nothing opening on screen. Connect Gmail once (Settings → Gmail: your own Google OAuth client, then Google's sign-in page; Shiki never sees your password) and "any new emails from Amazon?", "read Rahul's last email", "reply saying I'll be there" go straight through Gmail's API: searching and reading take a moment, drafts are saved directly, and sending always shows the whole email on a card first. The screen agent is now only for things that need clicking inside a site, and it's quicker.
- **What can Sky do?**: a dashboard page with everything the cat understands, grouped (your PC, Hindi and Hinglish, the web and email, files, documents, the screen, copy and remember, meetings, do it for me). Each line says what it does, "instant" marks the ones that run without Claude, and Try does it now (or starts the shortcut); the ones that need a file selected say so. A search box narrows the list as you type, and the shortcut keys follow your Settings.

## The cat's moods

**Alive, without the distraction** (cat menu → *Moves*: Lively, Calm (the default) or Still): now and then, when nothing's going on, it takes a few steps along the edge (paws tapping in turn, never far from home) or comes up for a stretch and a yawn: every minute or two when Lively, every few minutes when Calm, never when Still. It never does any of that while you're typing (input arriving while the pointer stays put), and while something runs full screen on its screen (a video, a game, a presentation) it steps out of sight altogether. While you talk to it, it comes up with wide, attentive eyes. When it opens File Explorer to show you something, it raises a paw toward that window instead of running across the screen. Dev aid: `Shiki.exe cat <listen|point|walk>`.

**It notices the PC too**: when the battery is low and unplugged, a little red battery floats up now and then and it gets sleepy sooner; plugging in gets a lightning bolt and a heart. When the processor has been flat out for about a minute, the odd drop of sweat. A finished download in Downloads (not partial files, not tiny ones) gets a green arrow and sparkles. Coming back after ten minutes away gets a hop and a heart. All of it is small and slow, from the corner.

**It grows**: points for files handled, requests to the cat, meetings (15 each), finished tasks (3) and days in a row (5) make levels (20, 60, 120, 200, 320, 480, 700, 1000, 1400 points, then every 500). Levels unlock cats (**Kuro**, black with golden eyes, at 2; **Ginger** at 4; **Ash** at 6) and things to wear (**bow tie** at 3, **headphones** at 5, which it also wears by itself while taking meeting notes, **party hat** at 7, **crown** at 10). A new level throws a party and says what it unlocked; pick cats and accessories in the card on Home. Dev aid: `Shiki.exe cat <battery|busy|plugged|download|back>`.

The cat hides below the taskbar edge with its ears and paws showing, and comes up when there's something to do. It also has moods:

- **Sleep.** After 5 quiet minutes (2 at night, 10 pm to 6 am) it slumps, lets go of the edge and Z's drift up. Hover to wake it: it pops up with a yawn. Any drop, job or shortcut wakes it too.
- **Big files.** Drop or start something over 1 GB and it's startled: wide eyes, a "!" and a hop.
- **Hard work.** It drums its paws while converting; past 8 seconds it squeezes its eyes shut and sweats, and a long job ends with a double hop and sparkles.
- **Petting.** Rub the mouse back and forth over its head: happy eyes and hearts. Six seconds straight is too much: a frown and a tail flick, and no petting for a moment.
- **Counter.** Its menu shows the tally under its name ("142 files converted, 1.3 GB saved"; saved counts only results that came out smaller). At 10, 25, 50, 100, 250, 500, 1,000 files and so on it throws a little party.

Dev aid: `Shiki.exe cat <sleep|startle|purr|grumpy|strain|party>` acts out a mood; `Shiki.exe catmenu` opens its menu at the mouse.

## Ways to use it

- **Select and press Ctrl+Alt+X.** Select files in File Explorer (any tab) or on the desktop, press Ctrl+Alt+X, and the wheel opens at the mouse. Press a wedge's number or click it; Tab switches pages. Change the shortcut in Settings.
- **Shift + drag.** Start dragging a file anywhere and hold Shift: the wheel opens right under the cursor. Drop the file on a wedge. To reach the tools, rest on the centre for half a second while dragging.
- **Drop on the cat.** Drag files onto the cat (even while it's hiding) and the wheel opens next to it. Click a wedge or press its number; click the centre, scroll, or press Tab to switch pages.
- **Right-click in Explorer.** Right-click a file → *Show more options* (or Shift + right-click) → **Convert with Shiki**. Select many files and they're handled as one batch (a multi-select "Merge" makes one PDF).
- **Double-click the cat**, or *Convert files…* in its menu, to pick files from a dialog.
- **Right-click the cat** or the tray icon for the menu: paste, snip, pick a colour, make a QR code, pick a cat, pick a look, Explorer menu, Shift + drag, start with Windows, quit.

- Lives in the bottom-left corner, sitting on the taskbar edge. Drag it sideways to move it along the edge.
- When there's nothing to do it ducks behind the edge, leaving only its ears and paws peeking over. It pops up when you hover it, Shift + drag a file, drop something on it, or a conversion is running.
- While converting it taps its paws; when a file is done it does a happy hop, then ducks again.
- Three cats to choose from in the menu: **Mochi** (calico), **Latte** (Siamese) and **Sky** (white and pale blue).
- The art is drawn as text grids in `UI/CatSprites.cs` (one character per pixel, poses are small patches on top). `Shiki.exe --export-sprites <folder>` writes every frame as PNG.

## Looks

Pick one in the menu:

- **Arcade** (default): pixel wheel with stepped, bevelled wedges and hard shadows, Pixelify Sans, square menus and toasts with a block progress bar. Everything is drawn on whole screen pixels so it stays sharp.
- **Glass**: Apple-style liquid glass. Each wedge, the hub, the menu and the toasts are separate glass pieces that bend what's behind them at their rims, with a rim light, slight frost, a saturation lift and a soft shadow. Segoe UI Variable. Follows the Windows light/dark setting.

### How the liquid glass works

`UI/LiquidGlass.cs` does on the CPU what the open-source web versions do with SVG displacement maps or WebGL shaders ([kube.io's write-up](https://kube.io/blog/liquid-glass-css-svg/), [liquidGL](https://github.com/naughtyduk/liquidGL), [liquid-glass-react](https://github.com/rdev/liquid-glass-react)):

1. Take a snapshot of the screen behind the popup (before it appears).
2. Describe each shape as a signed distance field (`GlassShapes`: circles, rounded rectangles, ring slices).
3. Give the rim a convex squircle profile, `f(x) = (1 − (1 − x)⁴)^¼`, and bend each ray by Snell's law (air 1.0 → glass 1.5). The sideways offset says where to sample the snapshot, so the rims bend the desktop and the middle stays clear. Red and blue are sampled slightly apart for a hint of colour fringing.
4. Frost a little, lift saturation, tint, then add a specular rim light from the upper left (and a softer one on the far side) plus a soft drop shadow.

**Live glass.** The glass follows what's behind it while a popup is open (a video playing, a window moving). Each glass window is excluded from screen capture (`SetWindowDisplayAffinity`, it stays visible on screen), so `LiveGlass` can keep grabbing the area behind it on a background thread. Everything that depends only on the shape (refraction offsets, rim light, shadow) is worked out once in a `GlassPlan`; each frame then only halves and frosts the snapshot and resamples it, and only when the background actually changed. Roughly 7 ms of maths plus about 15 ms for the Windows screen grab per frame, so 25–40 fps depending on size.

Side effect: while live glass is on, the wheel, menu and toasts don't appear in screenshots or screen recordings. Turn off *Glass follows what's behind* in the menu to get a still snapshot instead (and show up in recordings). For a frame dump while tuning, set `CONVERTEDBRO_GLASS_DUMP` to a folder.

The hovered wedge is lit in the colour of the file type: tangerine for images, iris for video, mint for audio, coral for PDF. Design tokens live in `UI/Theme.cs`.

## Build and run

Needs the .NET 8 SDK. On this machine it's installed at `%USERPROFILE%\.dotnet` (not on PATH).

```bash
~/.dotnet/dotnet.exe build src/Shiki/Shiki.csproj
```

```bash
~/.dotnet/dotnet.exe publish src/Shiki/Shiki.csproj -c Release -o publish/Shiki
```

FFmpeg is not in the repo. Put `ffmpeg.exe` and its DLLs in `tools/ffmpeg/` (the BtbN `win64-gpl-shared` build) and the build copies them next to the exe. The app also finds `ffmpeg.exe` on PATH.

### Installer

```bash
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File build/make-installer.ps1
```

This publishes the app and packs it with [Velopack](https://velopack.io) (MIT; `dotnet tool install -g vpk`) into `Releases/ShikiApp-win-Setup.exe` (about 120 MB, most of it FFmpeg and the AI runtimes). The version comes from `<Version>` in the project file, or `-Version 1.0.1`.

What Setup.exe does:

- Installs for the current user, no admin prompt, to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\ShikiApp` (the app's own data stays in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Shiki`), shows a splash with the cat, and installs the .NET 8 Desktop Runtime if it's missing.
- Adds **Shiki** to the Start menu, turns on *Start with Windows* (`--background`: just the cat at sign-in) and adds the Explorer right-click menu, then opens the dashboard.
- Registers an uninstaller in Settings → Apps. Uninstalling removes the app, the shortcut, the startup entry and the Explorer menu; settings, history, signatures and the background model stay, in case it comes back (delete `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Shiki` to remove them too).
- The build also writes the files Velopack needs for one-click updates later (`releases.win.json`, the `.nupkg`).

Setup.exe isn't code-signed, so a copy downloaded from the internet gets Windows SmartScreen's "Windows protected your PC" (More info → Run anyway). Signing needs a code-signing certificate (`vpk pack --signParams`). The bundled FFmpeg is the GPL build: fine for personal use; sharing the installer publicly means following the GPL (or switching to an LGPL build).

Command line (what the Explorer menu calls):

```
Shiki.exe                          start the cat + tray icon
Shiki.exe run <action-id> <files>  run one action, e.g. run video-mp4 "a.mov"
Shiki.exe menu <files>             show the format ring at the cursor
Shiki.exe paste                    give the clipboard to the cat
Shiki.exe pick                     pick a colour from the screen
Shiki.exe qr [text]                open the QR maker
Shiki.exe settings                 open Settings
Shiki.exe selected                 the wheel for what's selected in the Explorer window in front
Shiki.exe --register               add the Explorer right-click menu (current user, no admin)
Shiki.exe --unregister             remove it
```

Dev aids (run and exit, results in the log): `--export-sprites <folder>`, `--scan-test <photo>`, `--pdf-test <pdf>`, `--sign-test <pdf> <signature photo>`, `--form-test <exam|passport|visa|signature> <photo> [white]`, `--cutout-test <folder of jpgs>`, `--qr-test <text> <out.png>`, `--desktop-selection-test`, `--record-test <seconds> <out.mp4>`; `Shiki.exe record-demo <seconds>` runs the recorder on a fixed area (set `CB_SHOW_RECORDER=1` to let screenshots see its frame and bar).

Only one Shiki runs per user. Extra launches (Explorer starts one per selected file) pass their files to it over a named pipe, and they're batched for 0.7 s. When started from Explorer without the cat running, it works quietly and exits when done.

Logs and settings: `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Shiki\`.
